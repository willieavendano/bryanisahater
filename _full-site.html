The Man. The Myth.
The One Who Walked Out Early.

Bryan Deimer Muñoz — known to the eleven people who genuinely respect him as 8ry8ry — is Miami's self-appointed last line of defense against mediocre music, bad light, lazy edits, and DJ sets that "lack tension." A photographer by training, a techno DJ by ambition, a manager of things still to be determined, and a music critic by way of simply refusing to stop talking.

Picture George Costanza. Now give George Costanza a 50mm prime lens, a Moog synthesizer, a subscription to The Criterion Collection, and enough knowledge of Sopranos lore that he describes every personal conflict as "a sit-down." That is Bryan. He has strong opinions about your favorite DJ's kick drum. He has already explained them to you. You just weren't listening correctly.

Born and forged in Miami's sweltering nightlife underground, Bryan moves through the city like a slow-burning film noir protagonist who is perpetually three minutes late but arrives looking like he was always meant to be there. He has photographed sunsets and then, privately, offered the sun a note about its framing choices. He once told a headlining DJ — to their face, at the merch table, at volume — that their set was "competent." The DJ has not recovered. Neither has the merch table.

His relationship with music criticism is best described as a calling. Anthony Fantano reviews albums; Bryan reviews vibes, arcs, decisions. He does not give scores out of ten in the traditional sense — his scale is emotional, contextual, and highly personal, which is to say it is entirely made up on the spot and delivered with the authority of someone who has seen the full run of The Wire and considers it relevant to house music.

As a photographer, he captures Miami with clinical precision and a noir eye that sees everything as either deeply cinematic or deeply insufficient. His subjects: light, shadow, the human condition, the inside of Bardot, a specific corner of Wynwood at 2am, and whatever was in front of him when the light was "finally right." Editing style described by peers as "Kubrick if Kubrick had Lightroom and a grudge."

His techno DJ project — currently in what insiders call "the visionary phase" and what Bryan calls "in development" — is expected to debut at a venue befitting his taste and budget, which narrows it considerably. Early previews have been described by those present as "interesting," "dark," "where is this going," and "Bryan it is 2:30am and I have work tomorrow." He has taken all of this as encouragement.

He also manages. What, exactly, is fluid. But he manages it with the conviction of a man who has watched the Pilot of The Sopranos thirty-seven times and considers it load-bearing curriculum.

What Miami Says.

Unsolicited testimonials from the local music community. All quotes are spiritually accurate. Some are legally deniable.

Bryan came to my set at III Points and spent the entire time standing with his arms crossed, nodding slowly like a disappointed father watching his son attempt a free throw. When it was over he came up to me, looked me dead in the eye, and said "not bad." I've had those two words tattooed on my left forearm. It is the highest praise I have ever received from a human being at 3am.

David Sinopoli  ·  DJ / Producer  ·  Miami / Berlin  ·  Fabric Resident  ·  Innervisions

I sent Bryan a promo mix. He responded three days later with a four-hundred-and-twenty-word voice note. Not a text. A voice note. He used the word "pedestrian" four separate times. He also said, and I quote, that my kick drum was "philosophically incorrect." Not technically wrong. Philosophically. I still do not know what that means. I think about it every single time I open a DAW. He has fundamentally altered how I approach my work and I'm not sure if that's a good thing.

Danny Daze  ·  DJ / Producer  ·  Miami  ·  Clone Records / Omnidisc

He photographed my event. The photos were genuinely incredible — cinematic, moody, perfect. Afterwards he spent twenty minutes telling me the crowd had "no sense of release." I said Bryan, it's a techno night, that IS the vibe. He said "I know, and I'm concerned." I didn't know what to say. I still don't. The photos are framed in my office.

Anonymous Miami Promoter  ·  Wynwood  ·  Still Processing  ·  Photos Are Good Though

Bryan told me he liked my new track. Just "I liked it." No qualifications. No footnotes. No 'but the hi-hats.' Just: I liked it. I haven't slept properly since. Something must be wrong with it that he can't even find the words for. I've pulled it from release twice. My therapist says this is about Bryan. My therapist is right.

Miami Producer, Name Withheld  ·  Currently In Studio  ·  Reconsidering Everything  ·  All Because Of Two Words

He asked me — completely seriously, at a Sunday afternoon beach bar gig — if I had considered the "narrative arc" of my set. I was playing to twelve people at 2pm and someone had just spilled a mojito on the CDJ. I said Bryan, this is a beach bar on a Sunday. He said "all the more reason." He was still there when I finished. He said "better." I felt something.
